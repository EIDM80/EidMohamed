# DNA schema: the vocabulary for naming what a post does

Use these names in the DNA card so extractions can be compared across posts and saved in a DNA library. If a post does something that is not listed here, describe it in plain words and add it.

## 1. Text DNA

### Hook mechanic (lines 1-2, everything above the "see more" fold)

| Gene | What it does | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| `contrarian` | Contradicts a belief the audience holds | "Stop posting every day." |
| `before-after` | Two states with a time gap, and the gap is the curiosity | "2019: 47 rejections. 2026: I hire the people who rejected me." |
| `specific-number` | An odd, precise number signals a real story | "I sent 1,312 cold emails. 9 replied." |
| `confession` | Admits a failure or mistake the reader fears | "I lost our biggest client last month. My fault." |
| `open-loop` | Starts a story and withholds the outcome | "The CEO read my deck, closed his laptop, and said one sentence." |
| `direct-callout` | Names the reader and their situation | "If you run marketing at a university, read this before September." |
| `list-promise` | Promises a counted payoff | "7 prompts I use every morning:" |
| `pattern-interrupt` | A one or two word line that breaks the scroll | "No." / "Wrong." |
| `question` | A question the reader answers in their head | "Why do your best posts get 12 likes?" |
| `news-peg` | Attaches to something that just happened | "Google just changed AI search. Here's what it means for you." |
| `quote` | Leads with something someone said | "\"You're too old for digital,\" my manager said in 2012." |

Measure the hook's word count and whether line 2 escalates line 1 (stakes, a twist or a number) or merely continues it. Hooks that escalate on line 2 carry more of the load.

### Structure skeleton (the body)

Write the skeleton as a sequence of slots, for example `hook > context > struggle > turn > 3-point lesson > reframe > question`. Common skeletons:

- `story-lesson`: scene > tension > turn > lesson > question
- `listicle`: promise > N items (parallel phrasing) > summary line > CTA
- `myth-bust`: common belief > why it's wrong > what is true > proof > takeaway
- `framework`: named problem > named model (3-5 parts) > how to apply > CTA
- `behind-the-scenes`: what we did > what surprised us > numbers > what we'd change
- `hot-take-thread`: claim > 3 supporting points > the steelman of the other side > restated claim
- `carousel-teach`: cover promise > one idea per slide > recap slide > CTA slide

### Emotional arc

Choose one: `pain > relief`, `curiosity > payoff`, `outrage > agency`, `awe > practical`, `humility > authority`, `nostalgia > progress`. Mark the line where the turn happens (for example "turn at line 6 of 11"). A turn around 55-70% of the way through is common in story posts.

### Tension and turn

State the tension in one sentence (what is at stake) and the turn in one sentence (the reframe). If the turn cannot be written in one sentence, the post probably doesn't have one, and the list or the hook is carrying it.

### Voice markers

These are observed, not copied: sentence length (short and punchy, or flowing), person (I, we or you), register (formal, conversational, or dialect), signature devices (a repeated line, a callback to the hook, one-word paragraphs), humour type, and jargon level.

### Proof type

`personal-story`, `hard-number`, `client-case`, `screenshot`, `named-authority`, `research-cite`, `before-after-visual`, `none`. Posts with no proof rely on a strong contrarian hook or on identity ("people like us").

### CTA mechanic

`question` (to invite comments), `comment-keyword` (a lead magnet), `save-this`, `repost-if`, `follow-for`, `link-in-comments`, `dm-me`, or `none` (ends on the reframe). Note whether the CTA is easy (it can be answered in one word) or hard (it needs a story).

## 2. Visual DNA

- **Format:** text-only, single image, carousel or document (PDF), short video, poll, or article.
- **Layout:** centred bold statement, split before and after, a screenshot of a chat, tweet or DM, a photo with a text overlay, a diagram or framework, a checklist, or a quote card.
- **Text on image:** none, a headline only, or heavy (most of the message is on the image).
- **Faces:** the author's face, another person, or none. Note the expression and whether they look at the camera.
- **Colour and type:** high contrast or muted, warm or cool, one accent colour, serif or sans, weight.
- **Production level:** phone-native (raw, a selfie, a whiteboard photo), clean template, or designed. On LinkedIn, phone-native often signals authenticity. Note which one this post uses and whether it helped.
- **Carousel specifics:** slide count, words per slide, cover formula, and whether the last slide is a CTA.
- **Video specifics:** the first 2 seconds (the visual hook), captions yes or no, length, the cut rhythm, and talking head or B-roll.

## 3. Engagement DNA

- **Comment bait:** what makes replying easy (a one-word answer, taking a side, sharing their own number).
- **Identity signal:** who the post lets the reader say they are by liking or sharing it.
- **Shareability:** is there a line people would quote or screenshot?
- **Controversy level:** 0 (none) to 3 (divisive). Remixes for institutional accounts (a university or a company page) should stay at 1 or below.
- **Timing:** a news peg, a season (Ramadan, back to school, year end), or evergreen.
- **Author context:** follower count, niche authority, and whether the author usually performs at this level (the outlier test).

## 4. Load-bearing genes

After filling the card, pick the two to four genes that most plausibly explain the result and write one line each on why. Test: if you removed this gene, would the post still work? If yes, it is decoration.
