# Platform patterns

These are working heuristics, not guarantees. Platforms change ranking often, so treat numbers as starting points and check them against the user's own results.

## LinkedIn (default target)

- **The fold.** Only the first 2-3 lines (about 200 characters on desktop, fewer on mobile) show before "...see more". The hook must earn the click on its own. Put nothing important after the fold that the hook doesn't promise.
- **Length bands.** Short text posts run 40-120 words, story posts 120-250, and deep posts up to about 300. Longer isn't better; match the source DNA's band unless there's a reason not to.
- **Rhythm.** One idea per line, blank lines between beats, and no walls of text. Lists use parallel phrasing.
- **Links.** Posts with an external link in the body often get less reach. Put the link in the first comment, or say "link in comments", unless the user accepts the trade-off.
- **Hashtags.** 0-3 relevant ones at the end, or none. Hashtag walls read as spam.
- **Formats.**
  - Text-only works for stories and hot takes.
  - Single image works for a strong visual proof or a quote card.
  - Document or carousel (PDF) works for frameworks, checklists and step-by-steps. Use 6-12 slides with one idea per slide, a big cover title and a CTA on the last slide.
  - Short native video works for a talking head with captions. Most people watch muted, so captions are required.
  - A poll works for comment bait, but the reach is often weaker than people expect.
- **First comment.** Prepare one: the link, a resource, or a follow-up question. The author replying to early comments in the first hour tends to help.
- **Timing.** Weekday business hours in the audience's time zone. For a UAE and Gulf audience, working days are Monday to Friday, with Friday often lighter. Test Tuesday to Thursday mornings (08:00-10:00 GST) against the user's own data.
- **Company page vs personal profile.** Personal profiles usually reach much further. For an institution, a strong pattern is: the leader posts the personal story, and the page reposts with a comment.
- **Tone guardrails for institutional accounts.** Keep controversy at 1 or below. Avoid claims about competitors. Check the facts about programs, accreditation and rankings against the institution's official sources.

## X (Twitter)

- The hook is the whole first tweet. Threads use `1/` numbering sparingly.
- A single strong line plus an image often beats a thread.
- A LinkedIn story post usually needs to be cut to a 1-3 tweet version.

## Instagram

- Carousel is the default format for teaching. The first slide is the hook. Swipe-through drives distribution.
- Captions matter less than the visual. Put the key message on the image.
- Reels need a visual hook within the first 1-2 seconds, on-screen text, and trending audio where it fits (check the audio's licence for business accounts).

## TikTok and Reels

- The visual hook and the spoken hook land at the same time, in the first 2 seconds.
- Text overlay restates the hook.
- The pattern is fast cuts, captions, and a loop-back ending (the last line calls back to the first).
- Copy the format DNA (the camera angle, the cut rhythm, the reveal structure), never the audio of another creator's voice or their footage.

## Facebook

- Longer text is tolerated, and community and local angles work well.
- Native video and photo albums perform better than links.

## Converting between platforms

When the source is on one platform and the target on another, keep the load-bearing genes and rebuild the format for the target. For example, a TikTok "3 mistakes" video becomes a LinkedIn listicle post plus a 5-slide carousel, keeping the same mistake-reveal structure.
