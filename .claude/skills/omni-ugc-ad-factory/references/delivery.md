# Delivery — how a real person actually talks

This file exists because of one operator note that kills otherwise-perfect footage:

> *"She talks in a way no human will believe that's how she feels."*

The face was right, the skin was right, the light was right, the words were right. The
**delivery** was a performance. This is how you fix that.

---

## The two rules that matter more than anything else in this file

### 1. Give the STATE, not the choreography

Cues get performed. Conditions get inhabited.

| ✗ choreography (produces an actor) | ✓ state (produces a person) |
|---|---|
| "Creak on the word **day**." | "She runs out of air toward the end of a phrase, so her voice sags into a low creak on its own, without her intending it." |
| "Pause for 0.5s here." | "She stops because she is thinking, not for effect, and the gaps are uneven and slightly too long." |
| "Stumble on 'I get in bed'." | "She has not planned this sentence. She is finding the words while she says them." |
| "Sound frustrated." | "Her jaw is closer together than it should be, her words crowd up and speed up, and there is more push behind her breath than the volume needs." |

### 2. No emotion words in the delivery block

`actor-image.md` says it for stills — *emotion words produce performance; mechanics produce a
caught moment* — and it is **equally true for video**. Writing *"she is fed up"* hands the model
an emotion to play, and it will play it. Write the body and let the feeling fall out.

---

## The invariants — true of every register

Measured from a paralinguistic teardown of three high-engagement organic yappers (GRWM,
storytime, rant). These do not change with emotion:

- **Vocal fry / creak at the end of phrases.** The load-bearing tic. Across the corpus:
  *stayed, was, them, feelings, camera roll, bored, out of spite, here, It's April, Tuesday.*
- **Pauses of 0.3–0.6s between nearly every clause.** Occasional 0.9–1.5s. Constant, undramatic.
- **Pitch falls flat at the end of every line** and stays there.
- **An audible inhale before a new thought.**
- **The clip opens with a lip smack, then an exhale, and only *then* the first word.**
- **Never a laugh — a single exhale-laugh.** Appears in all three.
- **Repetition for rhythm.** *"tired as hell… tired as hell." "it snowed this week, it snowed on
  Tuesday." "Deuces, deuces, and deuces."*
- **Dropped subject pronouns.** *"Don't know if what I had was expired."*
- **Direct address to an absent target.** *"Sephora, I love y'all."*
- **Throwaway endings.** *"Anyways, that's it." "Anyways, bye babies."*
- **Deadpan asides drop in volume.** *"He was not laughing." "Isaac Newton better be lucky he's
  dead."*

> **Do not sample ASMR to build a corpus.** It is a *performed* register — deliberately slow and
> breathy — and it is the opposite of unguarded speech. Search GRWM, storytime, rant instead.

## The register-transposition method

**You will not have a corpus for every emotion, and you do not need one.** Decompose what you
have, then derive.

```
CORPUS  =  INVARIANTS  +  EMOTION-SPECIFIC MECHANICS
```

The corpus above is three *angry* yappers, so everything energetic in it is anger-specific.
Strip that out, keep the invariants, then **derive the new register from the physics of the
body**, not from a vibe.

### Anger (what the corpus gives you directly)

Crowding and speeding up · push behind the breath · tight jaw · incredulous direct address
(*"Like, what do you even mean"*) · hyperbole (*"fighting for my life"*) · repetition that
**escalates**.

### Defeat (derived, never researched — and it worked first try)

Anger has *push*, so the voice only collapses at the **end** of a phrase. Defeat has **no breath
support at all**, and every difference follows from that one fact:

- **Creak from the first word**, not just at phrase-ends. There is nothing holding the voice up
  to begin with.
- **Longer gaps.** The angry yappers are driving somewhere. She isn't.
- **The inhale inverts** — wet, shallow, hitched, instead of sharp.
- **The exhale-laugh becomes a sniff.** Same slot, no contempt in it.
- **Everything sits at aside-volume.** In anger only the aside drops; here the whole thing is
  muttered.
- **Voice cracks appear.** This marker never fired *once* across three angry yappers — it is the
  signature of the one thing they weren't doing.
- **Repetition deflates instead of escalating.** *"That's what it took. That's what it took"* —
  the second one smaller than the first.

**Use the same decomposition for any register you're handed.** Ask what the body is doing, and
the mechanics fall out.

## Writing the script for the register

The delivery block cannot save a script that is still *written*. See the humanize section in
SKILL.md step 4 — no closers, no antithesis pairs, no em dashes, one hard-to-fabricate specific,
and **one line of pure waste**, because real speech is never all load-bearing.

**Word budget:** ~25–28 words per 10s clip, and **drop to ~22–25 when the delivery is heavy with
disfluency** — breaths, gaps and hitches eat real seconds.

> **Empirical caveat.** The corpus says real rants are breathless **run-ons**, not clipped
> fragments, and on the page that is right. **Out loud, at ten seconds, the staccato version
> won.** Page truth and camera truth are not the same thing. When in doubt, generate both and let
> the operator pick.
